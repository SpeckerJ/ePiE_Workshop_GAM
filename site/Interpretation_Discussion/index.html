Interpretation & Discussion

The previous exercises have shown how influential the input parameters can be in driving the model results. However, predicting environmental concentrations of APIs and ePiE's structure rely on several key assumptions, which inherently introduce a certain level of uncertainty in the assessment. Accordingly, ePiE's results have to be properly interpreted. Some of these uncertainties we will briefly discuss briefly together.

Human Metabolism & Excretion

Exercises 3 & 4 highlighted the influence of human metabolism and the excreted fraction of an API after administration. This excreted fraction is the fraction of the unchanged API, meaning that there is a changed fraction. In other words, the API is metabolised, resulting in metabolites. These metabolites can still be biologically active and are excreted as well.

Which influence can these metabolites have on the environmental concentration of APIs?

Environmental Entry via WWTPs

ePiE assumes that all consumed APIs are either directly emitted to rivers and lakes, for the population fraction that is not connected to the sewer system, or that APIs travel through the sewer to WWTPs and are emitted to the aquatic environment.

How realistic is this assumption and how does it influences ePiE's results?

The UWWTD Database

ePiE considers only WWTPs connected to at least 2000 population equivalents. This is due to the fact that these WWTPs are not covered by the underlying UWWTD database, but these WWTPs still have an influence on the environmental concentrations of API.
